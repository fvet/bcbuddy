BC Buddy

Business Central, easier to work in

BC Buddy is a free browser extension for Chrome and Edge. It makes the Business Central web client easier to work in, gives every environment its own colour so nobody posts in production by mistake, and lets people report a problem to the helpdesk, with screenshots, without leaving BC. This deck shows where it sits in the architecture of Business Central, and what it changes for the people who use BC every day.

Business Central has three layers. At the bottom, the database: in the cloud Microsoft runs it and nobody changes it directly. In the middle, the business logic in AL: posting, validation, pages and reports. That is where partners and AppSource apps customise BC with extensions, which means development, testing and re-testing at every BC update. On top, everything that uses BC: the web client, the tablet and phone apps, Teams, Copilot and agents, MCP, APIs and SOAP. BC Buddy only touches one of those: the web client, inside each person's own browser. It adds no code to BC and has no effect on the other layers or on other users.

The first question is usually: does it touch our ERP? No. BC Buddy runs in the browser, on top of the page Business Central already shows. There is no extension to install in BC, no partner involvement and no upgrade risk. It has no account and no server, and it sends nothing anywhere: a problem report goes out through the person's own email app. Each person can switch every improvement on or off, and the web client improvements, the environment colours and Help and feedback each have one switch that pauses them. It is free, comes from the Chrome Web Store, and works in Chrome and Edge, for BC online and on-premises.

Copy values

Any value, copied in one click

Without BC Buddy

A No. or an amount is a link: clicking it opens a page instead of selecting it.

With BC Buddy

Point at a value,
click the copy icon,
paste it anywhere.

Copying a customer number or an amount out of Business Central is surprisingly hard: most values in a list are links, so clicking one opens a page instead of selecting it. With BC Buddy, resting the mouse on a value shows a small copy icon, circled here next to customer 20000. One click copies the value exactly as BC shows it, ready to paste into Excel, an email or Teams. It works in lists, in FactBoxes and on link fields on a card.

Maximize pages

Every page uses the full screen

Without BC Buddy

The card opens as a narrow window over the list.
Labels get cut off.

With BC Buddy

Every page opens in wide layout:
more room for fields, nothing cut off.

By default Business Central opens a card as a narrow window over the list you came from. Long labels such as Balance (LCY) As Vendor get cut off and you see fewer fields. BC Buddy opens every page in wide layout, and tile pages as a list. If someone switches a page back to narrow, BC Buddy respects that choice.

Actions always in reach

Without BC Buddy

The actions hide

With BC Buddy

Under the tabs of a page sits a row of actions such as Post and Release. Business Central often hides that row: you click a tab first, and only then see the actions. BC Buddy pins the action bar the first time a page opens, so the actions stay in view. Business Central remembers a page you pinned or unpinned yourself, and BC Buddy leaves that choice alone.

Dropdown size

Dropdowns that show every column

Without BC Buddy

Only No. and Description fit. The rest hides behind a sideways scrollbar.

With BC Buddy

As wide as its columns need: unit, price and inventory at a glance.

When you pick an item on a sales line, Business Central shows a small dropdown with room for two columns. Unit, price and inventory are there, but you have to scroll sideways to see them. BC Buddy makes the dropdown as wide as its columns need, so you choose the right item at once. Width and height each have a slider in the settings.

Week numbers in the date picker

Week numbers, right in the calendar

Without BC Buddy

Planning talks in weeks, but BC's calendar doesn't show them.

With BC Buddy

The week number in front of each week, counted the way BC counts weeks.

Delivery dates, production and holidays are planned in weeks, but the calendar of a date field in Business Central doesn't show week numbers. BC Buddy adds them in front of each week. They are counted the way Business Central counts weeks everywhere else: weeks start on Monday, and week 1 is the week with the first Thursday of the year.

On the lines of a sales order or in a journal, Business Central marks the line you are on with only a small arrow at the far left. On a wide line with many columns it is easy to lose your place. BC Buddy gives the whole line a light tint, from the first column to the last. The tint is deliberately soft so it doesn't distract while you type; the orange frame on this slide only points it out.

Keyboard shortcuts

The shortcuts for where you are, one click away

Business Central hides its shortcuts at the end of a button's tooltip. The BC Buddy icon in the top bar opens them in a side panel:

On this page: the page's own actions, such as Post (F9)

Where your cursor is: the lines, the list, the card or the date field

Anywhere: a few that always work, such as Alt+Q

The side panel sits next to Business Central, not on top of it, and changes along with the page.

Keyboard shortcuts save a lot of clicks, but Business Central keeps them out of sight: the only place it shows one is at the end of a button's tooltip, and the full list is a long article on Microsoft's site. BC Buddy adds its icon to the top bar, just before the gear. One click opens a side panel next to Business Central, and Keyboard shortcuts there shows the shortcuts that work where you are: the page's own actions, such as Post with F9, what works in the lines, the list or the date field you are in, and a few that work anywhere. The panel stays open, changes along when you open another page, and closes by itself when you leave Business Central. It needs no setting: it comes with the web client improvements.

Striped rows

Read across a wide list without slipping a row

Without BC Buddy

Every row is white. Halfway across, your eye slips to the next one.

With BC Buddy

Every other row a light grey, from the first column to the last.

In a wide list such as General Ledger Entries, every row is white, and by the time you read the amount at the far right it is easy to land on the row below. BC Buddy gives every other row a light grey, so your eye stays on the right line. The line you are on and the lines you select keep Business Central's own colours. The grey is deliberately light, so it is easier to see on a screen than on a projector. This one starts off: each person switches it on in the settings.

Hide the FactBox pane

Give the page the room, not the side pane

Without BC Buddy

The FactBox pane takes half the card. The fields squeeze into one column.

With BC Buddy

The pane stays closed. The fields use the whole width.

Cards, documents and lists open with the FactBox pane on the right: pictures, statistics, sales history. Handy now and then, but it takes the room the page itself needs. With this switch on, a page opens with the pane closed the first time. Need the details? The i button at the top right opens it, and Business Central remembers that for the page, so BC Buddy leaves it open there from then on. This one starts off: each person switches it on in the settings.

Environment colours

See at a glance where you are

Without BC Buddy

Test and production look exactly alike. Nothing says where you are.

With BC Buddy

Production in green: its name in the top bar,
a frame and a banner in the corner.

Without BC Buddy every environment shows the same top bar, so a test environment and the real one look exactly alike. One wrong tab and you post for real. BC Buddy colours the top bar per environment, with your own text, and can add a bar above it, a frame, a banner, a marking in the tab title and a coloured tab icon. You choose the colour per environment, per company or per customer. One person sets the colours up and shares them with the team in one file; everyone gets later changes automatically.

Help & feedback

Report a problem without leaving Business Central

The BC Buddy icon in the top bar opens Report a problem in a side panel next to Business Central:

Step by step: a line of text and a screenshot of each step, the whole screen or just a region

Point it out: an arrow, a pen or a box, and blur what others must not see

Email: the report opens in your own email app, with the company, the environment and a link to each page

IT sets the helpdesk address and the priorities once, for everyone.

When something goes wrong in Business Central, the helpdesk usually gets a vague email and has to ask which company, which page and what exactly happened. With Help and feedback, people report it straight from Business Central. The BC Buddy icon in the top bar opens a side panel next to the page. They write what they did one step at a time and take a screenshot of each step, of the whole screen or just the part that matters. On a screenshot they can draw an arrow, a pen line or a box, and blur names, amounts or anything else that is private. One click on Email opens the report in their own email app, with the screenshots inside, the environment and company, and a link to each page. Nothing goes through a server of ours. It starts off: IT switches it on and sets the helpdesk address, the priorities and the email subjects for everyone, through the team file or by policy.

Getting started takes three steps. Install BC Buddy from the Chrome Web Store; the web client improvements work at once, with nothing to set up. Then switch on the environment colours on the settings page: four sample rules are ready, with production in green and test environments in red, so production never looks like a test environment. Finally, export the settings to one file and share its address with your team, or let IT set it on every computer with Intune or Group Policy: everyone gets the same colours, web client and Help and feedback settings, including later changes. BC Buddy is free, installs nothing in Business Central, and every part has its own switch.
